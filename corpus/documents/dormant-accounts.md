# Dormant accounts

## What a dormant Kaskazi Bank account is

A Kaskazi Bank account becomes dormant when there has been no transaction started by you for 12 months.

Kaskazi Bank marks unused accounts as dormant to protect them. An account that nobody is watching is an easy target for fraud, so once an account is dormant, money can't be taken out of it until you reactivate it.

Each account is looked at on its own. If you use your Kaskazi Everyday Account every week but haven't touched your Kaskazi Goal Saver for 12 months, the Goal Saver becomes dormant and the Everyday Account doesn't.

## What counts as a transaction started by you

These count as transactions started by you, and each one restarts the 12 months on the account:

- a payment, transfer or withdrawal you make from the account;
- a card payment or cash withdrawal with your Kaskazi Debit Card;
- money you pay in yourself, as cash or by moving it from your other accounts;
- a payment made under a standing order you set up; and
- for a Kaskazi Fixed Deposit, an instruction you give about it, such as what happens at maturity.

These don't count, because you didn't start them:

- money paid in by someone else, including your salary, transfers from family, card and mobile money collections, and money from abroad;
- interest paid by Kaskazi Bank, and withholding tax deducted from it;
- Kaskazi Bank's fees and charges; and
- an automatic rollover of a Kaskazi Fixed Deposit.

Logging in to the app or checking your balance doesn't count either, because no money moves.

A Kaskazi Fixed Deposit isn't treated as inactive during the term you chose when you opened it. The 12 months start from its first maturity date if it then rolls over with no instruction from you.

### Dormancy timing example

The last transaction you started on your Kaskazi Goal Saver was a transfer in on 10 November 2026. Your interest is paid every month after that, but you start nothing yourself.

If you still haven't started a transaction on the Goal Saver by 10 November 2027, 12 months later, it becomes dormant.

## Before your Kaskazi Bank account becomes dormant

Kaskazi Bank sends you a message in the app before an account becomes dormant, so that you have a chance to use it. Making any transaction yourself, even a small transfer in from your other account, keeps it active for another 12 months.

## What still happens on a dormant account

A dormant account is still your account, and your money in it is safe. While an account is dormant:

- **money can still come in.** Salary, transfers and other payments in are accepted as usual.
- **interest is still paid.** A dormant Kaskazi Goal Saver keeps earning interest at its usual rate, paid monthly after withholding tax. A dormant Kaskazi Fixed Deposit keeps rolling over and earning interest.
- **fees still apply.** Monthly fees keep being charged on the account's usual terms.
- **statements carry on.** You still get your monthly statement in the app.

### Monthly fees on dormant accounts

| Account | Monthly fee while dormant |
|---|---|
| Kaskazi Everyday Account | KES 250, unless your salary is paid in that month or your average balance for the month is KES 50,000 or more |
| Kaskazi Student Account | KES 0 |
| Kaskazi Goal Saver | KES 0 |
| Kaskazi Business Account | KES 1,000 |
| Kaskazi Foreign Currency Account | USD 5, GBP 4 or EUR 5, depending on the currency |

### Example: fees using up a forgotten balance

You stop using a Kaskazi Everyday Account with KES 4,000 in it, no salary paid in and an average balance below KES 50,000. It becomes dormant after 12 months, but the KES 250 monthly fee is charged every month throughout. KES 4,000 ÷ KES 250 = 16, so after 16 months of fees the balance is used up.

If you don't need an account any more, it is better to close it than to leave it to become dormant.

## What you can't do while an account is dormant

While a Kaskazi Bank account is dormant, nothing can be taken out of it:

- payments, transfers and withdrawals from the account are refused;
- Kaskazi Debit Card payments and cash withdrawals from the account are declined;
- standing orders and other regular payments from the account are not made; and
- cheques drawn on the account are returned unpaid.

You can still log in and see the account and its balance in the app.

## Reactivating a dormant Kaskazi Bank account

You can reactivate a dormant account in either of these ways:

- **at any branch**, with your national ID card or passport; or
- **in the app**, with a selfie check, which compares a new selfie with the photo on your ID.

Reactivation is free. Kaskazi Bank charges KES 0 to reactivate an account.

Once the check is complete, the account works normally again straight away, and the 12 months start again from your next transaction.

If your details are out of date, for example your ID has expired or it is more than 3 years since you last confirmed your details, you'll be asked to update them when you reactivate. At a branch you can do both in one visit.

### Reactivating a joint or business account

For a joint Kaskazi Everyday Account, any one of the holders can reactivate it with their own ID or selfie check. The account's usual signing rules then apply as before.

For a Kaskazi Business Account, any signatory can reactivate it at a branch with their ID or in the app with a selfie check. Payments then follow the business's mandate as usual.

## Unclaimed money in dormant accounts

If an account stays dormant and Kaskazi Bank has no contact from you for a long time, the money may become unclaimed. After a long period with no contact, the law requires banks to hand unclaimed money to a government authority, from which you can still claim it.

The money is not lost if this happens. You, or the people entitled to your estate, can still claim it from that authority.

To make sure it never comes to that, keep your contact details up to date, and reactivate or close accounts you are no longer using.

## Keeping your Kaskazi Bank accounts active

To stop an account becoming dormant:

- start at least one transaction on it every 12 months, such as a small transfer in from your other account;
- set up a standing order into or out of it; or
- close any account you no longer need, and move the money to an account you use.

If you'll be away for a long time, for example working abroad, set up a standing order, or use the app to make a transaction on each account from time to time.

For help with a dormant account, ask the Kaskazi assistant or visit any branch.
