# Kaskazi Salary Advance

This guide explains the Kaskazi Salary Advance: who can get one, how much you can take, what it costs, and how it is repaid. It also covers the one-third rule and what happens if your salary does not arrive when expected.

Kaskazi Bank is invented, and this guide was written for a demonstration of a banking assistant. No real advance can be taken.

## What the Kaskazi Salary Advance is

The Kaskazi Salary Advance lets you receive part of your salary before pay day. It is meant for short-term needs that cannot wait until the end of the month, such as an unexpected medical bill, a school requirement or an urgent repair.

The Kaskazi Salary Advance works differently from a loan with monthly instalments:

- you pay a single flat fee of 3.0% of the amount you take, and **no interest**
- the whole advance is repaid in one go from your next salary, so it is cleared within a month at most
- you can have only one advance at a time

If you need a larger amount or more time to repay, the Kaskazi Personal Loan, which is repaid over 6 months to 60 months, may suit you better. It has its own guide.

## Who can get a Kaskazi Salary Advance

You can apply for a Kaskazi Salary Advance if:

- your salary is paid into your Kaskazi account, and
- your last 3 monthly salaries have arrived in your Kaskazi account in consecutive months, and
- you do not already have a Kaskazi Salary Advance that has not been repaid, and
- the advance you ask for passes the one-third rule explained below.

**Consecutive** means with no month missing. If your salary arrived in your Kaskazi account in July, August and September, you have 3 consecutive salaries. If it arrived in June, August and September, with nothing in July, you do not yet have 3 consecutive salaries, and you would qualify once October's salary arrives.

Salaries paid into an account at another bank do not count towards the Kaskazi Salary Advance, even if you can show us statements, because the advance is repaid directly from the salary as it arrives in your Kaskazi account. Statements from another bank can, however, help you qualify for a Kaskazi Personal Loan.

If you have just moved your salary to Kaskazi, you can apply once 3 salaries in a row have arrived. Having your salary paid into a Kaskazi Everyday Account also means its monthly fee of KES 250 is waived for that month.

## How much you can take as a Kaskazi Salary Advance

| | |
|---|---|
| Smallest advance | KES 1,000 |
| Largest advance | 50% of your net monthly pay, up to a maximum of KES 100,000 |
| Fee | 3.0% of the advance, flat, one-off |
| Interest | None |
| Repayment | In full from your next salary |
| Advances at a time | 1 |

Your net monthly pay is the salary that arrives in your Kaskazi account after tax and the other deductions your employer makes. We look at your recent salaries to work it out.

**Examples of the limit:**

- If your net monthly pay is KES 40,000, you can take up to KES 20,000.
- If your net monthly pay is KES 30,000, you can take up to KES 15,000.
- If your net monthly pay is KES 250,000, 50% would be KES 125,000, but the maximum Kaskazi Salary Advance is KES 100,000, so KES 100,000 is the most you can take.

The one-third rule, explained below, can bring your limit lower than 50% if your employer already deducts a lot from your pay.

## The Kaskazi Salary Advance fee: a flat 3.0%

The Kaskazi Salary Advance has one charge: a flat fee of 3.0% of the amount you take. There is no interest, no arrangement fee and no insurance. The fee is the same whether your next salary arrives in three days or in four weeks.

You receive the full amount of the advance in your account. The fee is collected together with the advance when your next salary arrives.

| Advance | Fee (3.0%) | Collected from your next salary |
|---|---|---|
| KES 1,000 | KES 30 | KES 1,030 |
| KES 10,000 | KES 300 | KES 10,300 |
| KES 25,000 | KES 750 | KES 25,750 |
| KES 50,000 | KES 1,500 | KES 51,500 |
| KES 100,000 | KES 3,000 | KES 103,000 |

A flat fee is easy to understand, but it adds up if you use an advance every month. Twelve advances of KES 10,000 in a year cost KES 3,600 in fees. If you find yourself needing an advance month after month, talk to us: a Kaskazi Personal Loan, or a plan to build some savings in a Kaskazi Goal Saver, may cost you less.

Because the fee is flat, an advance taken early in the month costs the same as one taken a few days before pay day. Take only what you need, though: whatever you take, plus the fee, comes out of your next salary before you can spend it.

## How a Kaskazi Salary Advance is repaid

A Kaskazi Salary Advance is repaid in full from your next salary. When that salary arrives in your Kaskazi account, we collect the advance and the 3.0% fee straight away, before you spend from it. You do not need to do anything.

For example, you take an advance of KES 15,000 on the 12th of the month and your salary of KES 40,000 arrives on the 28th. On the 28th we collect KES 15,450 (the advance of KES 15,000 plus the fee of KES 450), and KES 24,550 remains in your account.

You cannot spread a Kaskazi Salary Advance over more than one salary. You can repay it early, before your salary arrives, by paying in the advance plus the fee; the fee is the same however early you repay.

Once the advance and fee are repaid, you can take another advance if you still meet the conditions.

## The one-third rule for a Kaskazi Salary Advance

The one-third rule protects you from being left with too little to live on. After your Kaskazi Salary Advance and its fee are repaid from your salary, you must still take home at least one third of your basic pay.

**Basic pay** is the fixed salary on your payslip before allowances, overtime and deductions. **Take-home** here means your net pay arriving in your account, minus the advance and fee we collect from it.

The rule matters most when your employer already makes large deductions from your pay, for example for loans or other schemes, so that your net pay is low compared with your basic pay. We use your latest payslip to check your basic pay, and we ask you for it if we do not already have it.

To work out the most you can take under the one-third rule: take your net pay, subtract one third of your basic pay, and divide what is left by 1.03 to allow for the fee. Your limit is the lower of that figure, 50% of your net pay and KES 100,000.

### Worked example: when the one-third rule sets the limit

Wanjiru's basic pay is KES 90,000. After tax and her employer's other deductions, her net pay is KES 50,600.

- 50% of her net pay is KES 25,300.
- One third of her basic pay is KES 30,000, so after repaying the advance she must take home at least KES 30,000.
- Her net pay of KES 50,600 minus KES 30,000 leaves KES 20,600 to cover the advance and its fee. KES 20,600 divided by 1.03 is KES 20,000.

So the most Wanjiru can take is **KES 20,000**, not KES 25,300. If she takes KES 20,000, we collect KES 20,600 (the advance plus a fee of KES 600) from her next salary, and she takes home KES 30,000, exactly one third of her basic pay.

If Wanjiru asked for KES 25,300, we would collect KES 26,059 and she would take home only KES 24,541, which is less than one third of her basic pay. We would offer her the KES 20,000 instead.

### Worked example: when the one-third rule does not limit you

Otieno's basic pay is KES 60,000 and his net pay is KES 48,000.

- 50% of his net pay is KES 24,000.
- One third of his basic pay is KES 20,000.
- If he takes KES 24,000, we collect KES 24,720 from his next salary, and he takes home KES 23,280. That is more than KES 20,000, so the one-third rule is met.

So the most Otieno can take is **KES 24,000**, the 50% limit.

## One Kaskazi Salary Advance at a time

You can have only one Kaskazi Salary Advance at a time. You cannot take a second advance, or top up the first one, until the first has been repaid in full with its fee.

If you took a smaller advance than you were allowed and then need more before pay day, you have two choices: wait until your salary arrives and the first advance is repaid, or repay the first advance early (with its fee) and then apply for a new one. Each advance carries its own 3.0% fee.

## If your salary does not arrive

Sometimes a salary is late or does not come at all, for example if an employer pays late, you change jobs, or your salary is moved to another bank.

If your salary does not arrive, your Kaskazi Salary Advance is still owed. The advance plus a fee of KES 500 is collected from your Kaskazi account when money next comes in. This KES 500 fee is on top of the 3.0% advance fee. Whatever comes into the account, whether a late salary, a transfer or a cash deposit, is used to repay the advance first. If only part of the amount arrives, we collect what we can and the rest when more money comes in.

For example, you took an advance of KES 10,000. Your salary does not arrive on pay day. Two weeks later, KES 30,000 is paid into your account. We collect KES 10,800: the advance of KES 10,000, the 3.0% fee of KES 300 and the missed repayment fee of KES 500.

Because the Kaskazi Salary Advance needs your last 3 salaries to have arrived in consecutive months, a month with no salary also means you cannot take another advance until 3 consecutive salaries have come in again.

If you know your salary will be late, or that you are leaving your job, please tell us before pay day. We cannot always waive the fee, but we can talk through how the advance will be repaid. If your salary has stopped because you have lost your job, we will work with you on a way to repay that you can manage.

## Applying for a Kaskazi Salary Advance

You can apply for a Kaskazi Salary Advance in the app or through the assistant.

1. Tell us how much you need. The assistant can work out your likely limit from your recent salaries and explain the fee.
2. If we do not already have your latest payslip, we ask for it, so that we can check your basic pay for the one-third rule.
3. A member of our lending team checks the request and confirms it. The assistant can prepare the request and explain the rules, but it cannot approve or decline an advance.
4. Once confirmed, the advance is paid into your Kaskazi account in full. The app shows the amount, the fee, and that both will be collected from your next salary.

There is no charge to apply, and no fee if your request is declined. If we decline, we tell you why, for example because you do not yet have 3 consecutive salaries with us or because the one-third rule would not be met.

## Kaskazi Salary Advance: common questions

**Is there interest on a Kaskazi Salary Advance?** No. The only charge is the flat 3.0% fee, plus the KES 500 fee if your salary does not arrive and the advance has to be collected later.

**Can I get a Kaskazi Salary Advance if my salary goes to another bank?** No. Your salary must be paid into your Kaskazi account, with 3 consecutive monthly salaries already received.

**Can I have a Kaskazi Salary Advance and a Kaskazi Personal Loan at the same time?** Having one does not stop you applying for the other, but each is checked on its own rules, and we look at everything you owe before deciding.

**Does a Kaskazi Salary Advance affect my credit record?** Repaying on time from your salary, as normally happens, keeps your record clean. An advance that has to be collected late because your salary did not arrive is recorded as a late repayment.

**Can I talk to a person?** Yes. During staffed hours, 7:00 am to 10:00 pm every day, the assistant aims to hand you to a person within 5 minutes. Outside those hours, your conversation waits in the queue for the team. Our phone number and other contact details are not available in this demonstration.
