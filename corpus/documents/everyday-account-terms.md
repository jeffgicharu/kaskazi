# Kaskazi Everyday Account: terms and conditions

## About the Kaskazi Everyday Account

The Kaskazi Everyday Account is Kaskazi Bank's current account for individuals aged 18 and over. It is the account your salary goes into, the account you pay bills and send money from, and the account your Kaskazi Debit Card spends from.

These terms explain how the Kaskazi Everyday Account works: opening it, the monthly fee and how to avoid it, the debit card, cheque books, statements, what happens when there isn't enough money, joint accounts, closing the account and how the terms can change.

Kaskazi Bank is invented, and every figure here is sample data for a demonstration.

## Who can open a Kaskazi Everyday Account

You can open a Kaskazi Everyday Account if you are aged 18 or over. You need:

- your national ID card or passport;
- your KRA PIN;
- a phone number registered in your name; and
- a selfie, if you open the account in the app.

The phone number matters because Kaskazi Bank sends one-time codes to it, for example when you unblock your card. A one-time code is valid for 5 minutes.

If you are a student aged 18 to 25 with a valid student ID, the Kaskazi Student Account may suit you better, because it has no monthly fee.

## Opening a Kaskazi Everyday Account

You can open a Kaskazi Everyday Account in the Kaskazi app in about 10 minutes, or at any Kaskazi Bank branch.

There is no minimum opening balance. You can open the Kaskazi Everyday Account with nothing in it and pay money in later.

Your new account number has 13 digits. It starts with "0" and the 3-digit code of the branch the account belongs to. If you open the account in the app, you choose the branch you want it linked to.

Your Kaskazi Debit Card is ordered when the account opens. The first card is free.

## The Kaskazi Everyday Account monthly fee

The Kaskazi Everyday Account has a monthly fee of KES 250. It pays for running the account, the debit card, app and web banking, and your statements in the app.

Kaskazi Bank works out at the end of each calendar month whether the fee applies for that month. If either of the two waivers below applies, you pay nothing for that month. If neither applies, KES 250 is taken from the account at the end of the month.

You only need one waiver to apply. You never pay more than KES 250 for a month, and the fee is never charged twice for the same month.

If you never qualify for a waiver, the monthly fee comes to KES 3,000 over 12 months.

## Monthly fee waiver 1: your salary is paid into your Kaskazi Everyday Account

The KES 250 monthly fee is waived for any month in which your salary is paid into your Kaskazi Everyday Account.

Salary means pay sent to you by your employer. It counts for the calendar month in which it arrives in your account, whatever month it is pay for.

These payments do not count as salary:

- money you move yourself from another account, at Kaskazi Bank or elsewhere;
- money you pay in as cash, at a branch or an agent;
- money sent from a mobile money wallet; and
- payments from friends, family or customers of yours.

If you are self-employed or paid in cash, you won't qualify for the salary waiver, but you can still use the average balance waiver.

If your salary is split between two accounts, it still counts as long as part of it is paid into your Kaskazi Everyday Account by your employer.

## Monthly fee waiver 2: an average balance of KES 50,000 or more

The KES 250 monthly fee on the Kaskazi Everyday Account is also waived for any month in which your average balance is KES 50,000 or more.

### How Kaskazi Bank works out your average balance

Kaskazi Bank adds up the balance in your Kaskazi Everyday Account at the end of each day of the month, then divides by the number of days in the month.

Because it is an average, the balance can fall below KES 50,000 on some days and you can still qualify, as long as the average for the whole month is KES 50,000 or more. The reverse is also true: a high balance for a few days is not enough if the average comes out lower.

The balance at the end of the day is what counts. Money that comes in and goes out on the same day makes no difference to the average.

If you open the account part-way through a month, the average for that first month is worked out over the days the account was open.

## Kaskazi Everyday Account monthly fee examples

These examples use November 2026, which has 30 days.

### Example 1: salary paid in

Your employer pays your salary into your Kaskazi Everyday Account on 25 November. Your balance is low for most of the month.

The salary waiver applies, so you pay no monthly fee for November. Your average balance doesn't matter.

### Example 2: average balance above KES 50,000

You have no salary paid into your Kaskazi Everyday Account. Your balance is KES 70,000 at the end of each day from 1 to 20 November, then KES 15,000 from 21 to 30 November after you pay school fees.

Average balance: (KES 70,000 × 20 days + KES 15,000 × 10 days) ÷ 30 days = KES 51,666.67.

That is KES 50,000 or more, so you pay no monthly fee for November.

### Example 3: high balance for only part of the month

You have no salary paid into your Kaskazi Everyday Account. Your balance is KES 80,000 from 1 to 10 November and KES 30,000 from 11 to 30 November.

Average balance: (KES 80,000 × 10 days + KES 30,000 × 20 days) ÷ 30 days = KES 46,666.67.

That is below KES 50,000, so the monthly fee of KES 250 is charged for November, even though the balance was well above KES 50,000 for a third of the month.

### Example 4: a large payment in on the last day

Your Kaskazi Everyday Account balance is KES 45,000 from 1 to 29 November, with no salary paid in. On 30 November you pay in money, and the balance at the end of that day is KES 120,000.

Average balance: (KES 45,000 × 29 days + KES 120,000 × 1 day) ÷ 30 days = KES 47,500.

That is below KES 50,000, so the monthly fee of KES 250 is charged. Paying in a large amount at the end of the month does not usually lift the average enough.

### Example 5: opening the account part-way through the month

You open your Kaskazi Everyday Account on 16 November and keep KES 55,000 in it every day until 30 November.

The average for November is worked out over the 15 days the account was open, so it is KES 55,000. You pay no monthly fee for November.

### Example 6: salary paid late

Your employer usually pays your salary into your Kaskazi Everyday Account on 30 November, but this month the salary arrives on 1 December.

The salary counts for December, the month it arrives. For November, the fee is waived only if your average balance for November was KES 50,000 or more. If it wasn't, the KES 250 fee is charged for November.

## Interest on the Kaskazi Everyday Account

The Kaskazi Everyday Account does not pay interest, whatever your balance.

If you want your money to earn interest, you can move it to a Kaskazi Goal Saver or a Kaskazi Fixed Deposit. Moving money between your own Kaskazi accounts is free.

## Kaskazi Everyday Account cheque book

You can order a cheque book for your Kaskazi Everyday Account. A cheque book of 25 leaves costs KES 750, taken from the account when you order it.

You can order a cheque book in the app or at any branch, and collect it at the branch you choose.

Keep your cheque book somewhere safe and never sign a blank cheque. If your cheque book or a cheque is lost or stolen, tell Kaskazi Bank straight away so that the cheques can be stopped.

Only write a cheque if there will be enough money in your Kaskazi Everyday Account when it is presented. The Everyday Account has no overdraft, so a cheque that the balance cannot cover is returned unpaid.

## Kaskazi Everyday Account statements

You get a monthly statement for your Kaskazi Everyday Account in the app. Statements in the app are free, and you can download them whenever you need them.

If you need a printed statement, for example for an embassy application or a landlord, you can ask for one at any branch. A printed statement costs KES 300 per statement.

Check your statement each month. If you see a card payment you don't recognise, report it within 60 days of the transaction date so that Kaskazi Bank can investigate it as a dispute.

## When there isn't enough money in your Kaskazi Everyday Account

The Kaskazi Everyday Account has no overdraft. Kaskazi Bank does not lend you money to cover a payment, so a payment only goes through if there is enough money in the account to cover it and any fee for it.

### Card payments and cash withdrawals

If you try to pay by card or withdraw cash and there isn't enough money in your Kaskazi Everyday Account, the payment or withdrawal is declined. Nothing is taken from the account.

### Transfers, bill payments and standing orders

A transfer or bill payment you make in the app is refused straight away if the balance can't cover it and its fee. You can try again once you have paid money in.

If a standing order is due and there isn't enough money in your Kaskazi Everyday Account, it is not paid that day. Kaskazi Bank sends you a message so that you can make the payment yourself. A standing order that isn't paid is not charged the KES 50 standing order fee.

If a standing order is not paid, the person you were paying is not paid either, so make sure they know and pay them another way if you need to.

### Cheques

If a cheque you wrote is presented and your Kaskazi Everyday Account can't cover it, the cheque is returned unpaid to the person who paid it in. Kaskazi Bank does not pay part of a cheque.

A returned cheque can cause you real problems with the person you paid, so check your balance before you write one.

### Fees when your balance is low

Kaskazi Bank's own fees, such as the KES 250 monthly fee or the KES 750 for a cheque book, are taken from your Kaskazi Everyday Account when they are due.

If your balance is lower than a fee when it is due, Kaskazi Bank takes what is in the account, and the rest of the fee is shown as owing. The amount owing is taken as soon as money next comes into the account. Your balance never goes below zero.

## Joint Kaskazi Everyday Accounts

You can open a Kaskazi Everyday Account jointly with other people, for example a spouse or a family member. Joint accounts are opened at a branch.

### Who can be a joint holder

Every holder of a joint Kaskazi Everyday Account must be aged 18 or over and must bring the same documents as a single applicant: a national ID card or passport, a KRA PIN and a phone number registered in their own name.

### How joint holders sign

When you open a joint Kaskazi Everyday Account, all of you choose how the account can be used:

- **any holder alone:** each of you can make payments, write cheques and use the account on your own; or
- **all holders together:** every payment needs the agreement of all of you.

You can change the choice later, but only if all holders agree.

Each joint holder can have their own Kaskazi Debit Card. A card spends from the joint account, so every holder can see and use the same balance.

### Joint holders' responsibilities

Every holder of a joint Kaskazi Everyday Account is responsible for the whole account, not just a share of it. That includes the monthly fee and any amount owing.

For a joint account, the monthly fee is waived if any holder's salary is paid into the account that month, or if the average balance for the month is KES 50,000 or more.

### If a joint holder dies

If a joint holder dies, the money in the joint Kaskazi Everyday Account belongs to the surviving holder or holders, who can keep using the account. Kaskazi Bank will need to see the death certificate.

### If joint holders disagree

If Kaskazi Bank learns that joint holders are in dispute, it may require all holders to agree to every payment until the dispute is settled, even if the account was set up for any holder alone.

A joint holder can only be removed from the account if all holders agree.

## Closing your Kaskazi Everyday Account

You can close your Kaskazi Everyday Account at any time, at any branch or in the app.

Before you close it:

- move your salary and any regular payments in to your new account;
- cancel or move your standing orders and any card payments you have set up, such as subscriptions;
- make sure any cheques you have written have been paid, because cheques presented after the account closes are returned unpaid; and
- return or destroy any unused cheques.

When the account closes, any fee owing is taken first. Kaskazi Bank then pays the remaining balance to another account you choose, or gives it to you in cash at a branch. Your Kaskazi Debit Card is cancelled.

Closing the account does not end any loan or credit card you have with Kaskazi Bank. If your loan repayments are collected from the Everyday Account, arrange another account to pay them from first.

To close a joint Kaskazi Everyday Account, all holders must agree.

Kaskazi Bank keeps the records of a closed account for 7 years after it closes.

### When Kaskazi Bank may close your account

Kaskazi Bank may close your Kaskazi Everyday Account by giving you notice in writing first, for example if the account has had no money in it for a long time.

Kaskazi Bank may close the account straight away, without notice, if it reasonably believes the account is being used for fraud or another crime, or if you have given false information to open it.

## If you stop using your Kaskazi Everyday Account

If you don't start any transaction on your Kaskazi Everyday Account for 12 months, the account becomes dormant. Payments out stop, but the monthly fee still applies in any month where neither waiver applies.

You can reactivate a dormant account at any branch with your ID, or in the app with a selfie check. Reactivation is free. The Dormant accounts document explains the details.

## Keeping your Kaskazi Everyday Account details up to date

Kaskazi Bank asks you to confirm your details every 3 years, and whenever something changes, such as a new phone number or a renewed ID.

If your details are out of date, Kaskazi Bank may limit what you can do with the account until you update them. You can update most details in the app or at a branch.

## Changes to the Kaskazi Everyday Account terms

Kaskazi Bank may change these terms, including the monthly fee, the waivers and the debit card fees and limits.

If a change is to your disadvantage, Kaskazi Bank will tell you before it takes effect, by a message in the app and a notice at branches, giving you reasonable notice. If you don't agree with the change, you can close your Kaskazi Everyday Account before it takes effect.

Changes that are to your advantage, such as a lower fee, may take effect straight away. Kaskazi Bank will tell you about them afterwards.

The current terms are always available in the app and at branches. These terms are version 1.0 and took effect on 1 October 2026.
