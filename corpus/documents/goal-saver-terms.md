# Kaskazi Goal Saver: terms and conditions

## About the Kaskazi Goal Saver

The Kaskazi Goal Saver is Kaskazi Bank's savings account for individuals aged 18 and over. It pays interest on your whole balance, works out that interest every day and pays it monthly. You can divide your savings into up to 5 named goals, such as school fees, a deposit for a plot or an emergency fund.

The Kaskazi Goal Saver is for saving, not for spending. It has no debit card and gives you one free withdrawal each month.

Kaskazi Bank is invented, and every figure here is sample data for a demonstration.

## Who can open a Kaskazi Goal Saver

You can open a Kaskazi Goal Saver if you are aged 18 or over. You need the same documents as for any Kaskazi Bank personal account: your national ID card or passport, your KRA PIN and a phone number registered in your name, plus a selfie if you open the account in the app.

You can open a Kaskazi Goal Saver in the app or at any branch. You need to pay in at least KES 1,000 to open it.

The KES 1,000 minimum applies only when you open the account. Afterwards, your balance can fall below KES 1,000 without any charge, and the account stays open.

There is no monthly fee for the Kaskazi Goal Saver.

## Goals in your Kaskazi Goal Saver

You can divide the money in your Kaskazi Goal Saver into up to 5 goals. Each goal has a name you choose, and you can give it a target amount and a target date if you like. The app shows how close each goal is to its target.

Goals are a way of organising your money inside one account. They are not separate accounts. All your goals together make up your Kaskazi Goal Saver balance, and that whole balance is what earns interest.

Moving money from one goal to another is free and does not count as a withdrawal. You can do it as often as you like.

If you want a sixth goal, you need to close or rename one of your existing goals first. When you close a goal, you choose another goal to move its money to. Closing a goal does not move money out of the account, so it is not a withdrawal.

Money that you haven't given to a goal sits in your Goal Saver balance as unallocated money. It earns interest in exactly the same way.

## How Kaskazi Goal Saver interest rates work

The Kaskazi Goal Saver pays one rate on your whole balance. Which rate depends on how much is in the account:

- if your balance is KES 10,000 or more, the whole balance earns 5.0% a year;
- if your balance is below KES 10,000, the whole balance earns 3.0% a year.

The rate is not split. With a balance of KES 15,000, all KES 15,000 earns 5.0% a year. Kaskazi Bank does not pay 3.0% on the first KES 10,000 and 5.0% on the rest.

### A balance of exactly KES 10,000

A balance of exactly KES 10,000 earns the higher rate, 5.0% a year. A balance of KES 9,999 earns 3.0% a year on the whole KES 9,999.

The threshold applies to the balance of the whole Kaskazi Goal Saver, across all your goals, not to each goal separately.

## How Kaskazi Goal Saver interest is calculated each day

Kaskazi Bank calculates Goal Saver interest every day, on the balance at the end of the day (the closing balance).

Each day's interest is: closing balance × the rate for that day ÷ 365.

The rate for each day depends on that day's closing balance. If your balance is KES 10,000 or more at the end of a day, that day earns 5.0% a year. If it is below KES 10,000 at the end of a day, that day earns 3.0% a year. So a withdrawal that takes you below KES 10,000 changes the rate from that day on, and paying in enough to reach KES 10,000 again changes it back.

Money paid in and taken out on the same day does not earn interest for that day, because only the closing balance counts.

Daily interest is added up through the month and the total is rounded to the nearest cent when it is paid.

## When Kaskazi Goal Saver interest is paid

Kaskazi Goal Saver interest is paid monthly, on the last working day of the month. It is paid into your Goal Saver, after withholding tax has been deducted.

Interest is shared between your goals and any unallocated money in proportion to how much each held, so every goal grows with its share.

If the last day of the month falls on a weekend or public holiday, interest is paid on the last working day before it. Interest for the days after that payment, up to the end of the month, is included in the next month's payment. For example, January 2027 ends on a Sunday, so January's interest is paid on Friday 29 January, and the interest for 30 and 31 January is paid with February's interest.

Because interest is added to your balance each month, next month's interest is worked out on a balance that includes it.

## Withholding tax on Kaskazi Goal Saver interest

Withholding tax of 15.0% is deducted from Kaskazi Goal Saver interest before it is paid to you. Kaskazi Bank pays the tax to the tax authority on your behalf.

You receive the interest after tax. Your statement shows three figures for each payment: the interest earned (gross), the tax deducted, and the interest paid to you (net).

Withholding tax is worked out on the total interest for the payment, not day by day.

## Kaskazi Goal Saver interest examples

These examples use November 2026, which has 30 days. Interest for November is paid on Monday 30 November, the last working day of the month. Figures are rounded to the nearest cent.

### Example 1: a full month at KES 120,000

Your Kaskazi Goal Saver balance is KES 120,000 at the end of every day in November. That is KES 10,000 or more, so the whole balance earns 5.0% a year.

- Interest each day: KES 120,000 × 5.0% ÷ 365 = about KES 16.44
- Interest for November (gross): KES 120,000 × 5.0% × 30 ÷ 365 = KES 493.15
- Withholding tax at 15.0%: KES 73.97
- Interest paid to you (net): KES 419.18

### Example 2: crossing KES 10,000 part-way through the month

Your Kaskazi Goal Saver balance is KES 8,000 at the end of each day from 1 to 15 November. On 16 November you pay in KES 10,000, so your balance is KES 18,000 at the end of each day from 16 to 30 November.

- 1 to 15 November, below KES 10,000, so 3.0% a year: KES 8,000 × 3.0% × 15 ÷ 365 = KES 9.86
- 16 to 30 November, KES 10,000 or more, so 5.0% a year: KES 18,000 × 5.0% × 15 ÷ 365 = KES 36.99
- Interest for November (gross): KES 46.85
- Withholding tax at 15.0%: KES 7.03
- Interest paid to you (net): KES 39.82

### Example 3: KES 10,000 compared with KES 9,999

Over the 30 days of November, in a Kaskazi Goal Saver:

- a balance of KES 10,000 earns 5.0% a year: KES 10,000 × 5.0% × 30 ÷ 365 = KES 41.10 gross;
- a balance of KES 9,999 earns 3.0% a year: KES 9,999 × 3.0% × 30 ÷ 365 = KES 24.66 gross.

One shilling less in the account means about KES 16 less interest for the month, because the whole balance moves to the lower rate. If you are close to KES 10,000, it is worth topping up.

## Withdrawals from your Kaskazi Goal Saver

You get 1 free withdrawal from your Kaskazi Goal Saver each calendar month. Each extra withdrawal in the same month costs KES 150.

A withdrawal is any time money leaves the Goal Saver: a transfer to another of your accounts, or cash taken at a branch. These are not withdrawals:

- moving money between your goals;
- closing a goal and moving its money to another goal;
- interest being paid in, and withholding tax being deducted.

The free withdrawal does not carry over. If you don't use it in October, you still get only 1 free withdrawal in November.

The KES 150 fee is taken from your Goal Saver at the time of the extra withdrawal. You need enough in the account to cover both the withdrawal and the fee.

The fee is the same whatever the size of the withdrawal: an extra withdrawal of KES 500 costs KES 150, and so does an extra withdrawal of KES 50,000.

### Kaskazi Goal Saver withdrawal examples

You take money out of your Goal Saver on 3 November, 12 November and 27 November. The first withdrawal is free. The second and third cost KES 150 each, so you pay KES 300 in withdrawal fees for November. On 1 December your free withdrawal starts again.

If you know you will need money twice in a month, take it out in one withdrawal instead. One withdrawal of KES 20,000 is free; two withdrawals of KES 10,000 in the same month cost KES 150.

### Where Kaskazi Goal Saver withdrawals can go

You can move money from your Kaskazi Goal Saver to another Kaskazi account in your own name, such as your Kaskazi Everyday Account, in the app or at a branch. Transfers between your own Kaskazi accounts are free; only the withdrawal fee can apply.

You can also take cash from your Goal Saver at any branch. That counts as a withdrawal in the same way.

You can't pay other people, other banks or mobile money wallets directly from the Goal Saver. Move the money to your Kaskazi Everyday Account first and pay from there.

## No debit card on the Kaskazi Goal Saver

The Kaskazi Goal Saver does not come with a debit card, and you can't link one to it. You can't use the Goal Saver at ATMs, at Kaskazi agents for withdrawals, or for card payments in shops or online.

This is deliberate. Keeping your savings away from your card makes them harder to spend on impulse and keeps them safer if your card is lost or stolen.

To spend money from your savings, move it to your Kaskazi Everyday Account and use your Kaskazi Debit Card from there.

## Paying into your Kaskazi Goal Saver

You can pay into your Kaskazi Goal Saver at any time, with any amount:

- by transfer from your other Kaskazi accounts in the app, which is free;
- as cash at a branch or at a Kaskazi agent (agent deposits are free); or
- with a standing order from your Kaskazi Everyday Account, so that you save the same amount every month. Each standing order payment costs KES 50.

When you pay in, you choose which goal the money goes to. If you don't choose, it is held as unallocated money until you do.

Paying in never counts as a withdrawal and never has a Goal Saver fee.

## Kaskazi Goal Saver statements

You get a monthly statement for your Kaskazi Goal Saver in the app, free. It shows money paid in, withdrawals, any withdrawal fees, and the gross interest, withholding tax and net interest for the month.

A printed statement at a branch costs KES 300 per statement.

## If you stop using your Kaskazi Goal Saver

If you don't start any transaction on your Kaskazi Goal Saver for 12 months, it becomes dormant. Interest is still calculated and paid while it is dormant, but you can't take money out until you reactivate it.

Interest payments are made by Kaskazi Bank, so they don't count as a transaction started by you and don't stop the account becoming dormant.

You can reactivate a dormant Goal Saver at any branch with your ID, or in the app with a selfie check. Reactivation is free.

## Closing your Kaskazi Goal Saver

You can close your Kaskazi Goal Saver at any time, in the app or at a branch.

When you close it, Kaskazi Bank works out the interest earned since the last payment, deducts withholding tax at 15.0%, and pays the net interest with your balance into your Kaskazi Everyday Account or another account you choose.

Closing the account moves your money out, so it counts as a withdrawal. If you have already used your free withdrawal that month, the KES 150 fee applies to the closing withdrawal. To avoid it, close the account in a month in which you haven't yet made a withdrawal.

## Changes to the Kaskazi Goal Saver terms and interest rates

Kaskazi Bank may change the Kaskazi Goal Saver interest rates, the KES 10,000 threshold, the withdrawal fee and these terms.

If a change is to your disadvantage, such as a lower interest rate, Kaskazi Bank will tell you in the app before it takes effect, giving you reasonable notice. You can move your money out or close the account before the change if you don't want to accept it.

A change to your advantage, such as a higher rate, may take effect straight away.
