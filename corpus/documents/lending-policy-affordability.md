# How we decide on a loan

This guide explains how Kaskazi Bank decides whether to lend to you, and how much. It covers the affordability rule with worked examples, the other checks we make, the common reasons applications are declined, and how to ask for a decision to be reviewed. It applies to the Kaskazi Personal Loan. The Kaskazi Salary Advance has its own simpler rules, set out in its own guide.

Kaskazi Bank is invented, and this guide was written for a demonstration of a banking assistant. No real lending decisions are made.

## Our approach to lending decisions

We want every loan we make to be one you can repay without hardship. A loan that stretches you too far is bad for you, because missed payments damage your credit record and add fees, and it is bad for us. So we look at what you earn, what you already owe, and how you have handled borrowing before.

We apply the same rules to everyone. We do not decide on the basis of your tribe, religion, gender, marital status, disability or where you come from. Two customers with the same pay, the same existing loans and the same record should get the same answer.

A person in our lending team makes every decision. The banking assistant can explain these rules, work out whether a loan is likely to fit, and prepare your application, but it cannot approve or decline a loan.

## The affordability rule: 40% of net monthly pay

The main rule for a Kaskazi Personal Loan is the affordability rule:

**All your monthly loan repayments, including the new Kaskazi Personal Loan instalment, must come to no more than 40% of your net monthly pay.**

The rest of your pay, at least 60%, stays free for rent, food, transport, school fees and everything else. We do not make exceptions to the 40% limit, because it is there to protect you.

The affordability rule sits alongside the other conditions for a Kaskazi Personal Loan: a net monthly pay of at least KES 25,000, at least 3 months of salary history, an age of at least 21 when you apply and no older than 65 when the loan ends, a loan of KES 50,000 to KES 3,000,000, and a term of 6 months to 60 months.

### What counts as net monthly pay

Net monthly pay is the salary you receive each month after tax and the other deductions your employer makes by law. We take it from your latest payslips and check it against your account statements.

- If your pay varies, for example with overtime or commission, we use the amount you can show regularly over your latest payslips, not your best month.
- One-off payments, such as a bonus or arrears of pay, are not counted as regular pay.
- If your employer deducts a loan repayment from your pay, we add that deduction back to work out your net monthly pay, and then count it as a loan repayment. That way a loan is treated the same whether your employer collects it or the lender does. Worked example 4 shows how this works.

### What counts as a monthly loan repayment

We count every regular loan repayment you make, to us or to anyone else, including:

- other Kaskazi loans
- loans from other banks
- loans your employer deducts from your payslip
- loans from savings and credit cooperatives
- mobile loans and digital loans
- for a credit card, the minimum monthly payment due

If you are using the new Kaskazi Personal Loan to pay off another loan in full, tell us when you apply. We can leave that loan's repayment out of the calculation, as long as the loan is cleared from the new money and you show us proof that it has been closed.

## Worked example 1: no other loans

Amina's net monthly pay is KES 80,000 and she has no other loans. She asks for a Kaskazi Personal Loan of KES 500,000 over 36 months.

- 40% of KES 80,000 is KES 32,000. That is the most all her loan repayments can come to each month.
- The instalment on KES 500,000 over 36 months at 15.5% a year is KES 17,455.
- KES 17,455 is less than KES 32,000, so the loan fits. It takes about 22% of her net pay.

The largest loan Amina could take with no other loans is KES 916,639 over 36 months, or KES 1,330,405 over 60 months, because those are the largest amounts whose instalments stay within KES 32,000.

## Worked example 2: an existing loan means you can borrow less

Brian's net monthly pay is also KES 80,000, but he already repays KES 15,000 a month on a loan with another bank. He asks for a Kaskazi Personal Loan of KES 600,000 over 36 months.

- 40% of KES 80,000 is KES 32,000.
- His existing repayment is KES 15,000, so there is KES 17,000 a month left for a new instalment.
- The instalment on KES 600,000 over 36 months is KES 20,946.
- His total repayments would be KES 15,000 plus KES 20,946, which is KES 35,946, or about 45% of his net pay.

That is over the 40% limit, so we cannot lend KES 600,000 over 36 months. Brian has three choices:

- **Borrow less over the same term.** The largest loan over 36 months with an instalment of no more than KES 17,000 is KES 486,971.
- **Take a longer term.** KES 600,000 over 48 months has an instalment of KES 16,851, bringing his total to KES 31,851, which fits. Over 60 months it is KES 14,432, a total of KES 29,432. A longer term costs more interest overall.
- **Wait until his existing loan is smaller or repaid,** and apply again.

While his existing loan stays as it is, the largest Kaskazi Personal Loan Brian can have is KES 706,787 over 60 months, the longest term.

## Worked example 3: finding the largest loan that fits

You can work out the largest Kaskazi Personal Loan that fits the affordability rule in three steps.

1. **Find your limit.** Multiply your net monthly pay by 40%.
2. **Find the room left.** Subtract all your existing monthly loan repayments.
3. **Turn the room into a loan amount** for your chosen term, using the reducing-balance formula: largest loan = room × (1 − (1 + r) to the power of −n) ÷ r, where r is the monthly rate (15.5% divided by 12) and n is the number of months.

Using Brian's room of KES 17,000 a month:

| Term | Largest loan | Instalment |
|---|---|---|
| 36 months | KES 486,971 | KES 17,000 |
| 48 months | KES 605,326 | KES 17,000 |
| 60 months | KES 706,787 | KES 17,000 |

The largest loan is also capped at KES 3,000,000, and the term at 60 months, whatever your pay. The assistant can do this calculation for you for any term. The figure it gives is a guide to what is likely to fit, not an offer: a person still checks your documents and your credit record before deciding.

## Worked example 4: a loan your employer deducts from your pay

Chebet's payslip shows net pay of KES 62,000 after her employer deducts KES 8,000 a month for a loan.

- We add the KES 8,000 back, so her net monthly pay for the affordability rule is KES 70,000.
- 40% of KES 70,000 is KES 28,000.
- Her existing repayment is KES 8,000, so there is KES 20,000 a month for a new instalment.

She asks for KES 300,000 over 24 months. The instalment is KES 14,617, making her total repayments KES 22,617, which is within KES 28,000. The loan fits. The largest she could borrow would be KES 410,481 over 24 months, or KES 572,904 over 36 months.

## Worked example 5: at the minimum net pay

David's net monthly pay is exactly KES 25,000, the minimum for a Kaskazi Personal Loan, and he has no other loans.

- 40% of KES 25,000 is KES 10,000.
- The largest loan he could take is KES 110,509 over 12 months, KES 286,459 over 36 months, or KES 415,766 over 60 months.

If David's net pay were KES 24,000, he could not have a Kaskazi Personal Loan at all, even a small one that would fit within 40%, because his pay would be below the KES 25,000 minimum.

## Other things we check before deciding on a loan

The affordability rule is necessary but not enough on its own. Before deciding on a Kaskazi Personal Loan, a member of our lending team also checks the following.

### Your pay and salary history

We check that your salary has been paid regularly for at least 3 months. If it is paid into a Kaskazi account, we can see this ourselves. If it is paid elsewhere, we look for it on 6 months of statements from that bank. We check that the net pay on your payslips is the amount that actually reaches your account.

### Your existing loans and your credit record

We check your record with a credit reference bureau. A credit reference bureau collects information from lenders about the loans people have and how they repay them. Your report shows us loans you may not have mentioned, whether you have repaid on time, and whether any loan is in arrears or has been written off. In this demonstration the check is simulated and no real bureau is contacted.

A past problem does not automatically mean a decline. A late payment some time ago that has since been cleared counts far less than a loan in default today.

### Your documents

We check that your payslips, statements, ID and KRA PIN are genuine, complete and up to date, that the figures add up, and that your name matches on every document. Our guide "Applying for a loan: steps and documents" explains what makes a document acceptable.

### Your age and the term

You must be at least 21 when you apply and no older than 65 when the loan ends. If you are close to that age, we may offer a shorter term than you asked for.

### How you have banked with us

If you already bank with us, we look at how you have managed your accounts and any loans with us, for example whether instalments have been paid on time.

## Why loan applications are declined

The most common reasons a Kaskazi Personal Loan application is declined are:

- **Affordability:** your repayments, including the new loan, would be over 40% of your net monthly pay, and you did not want a smaller amount or a longer term.
- **Pay below the minimum:** your net monthly pay is under KES 25,000.
- **Not enough salary history:** we could not see at least 3 months of regular salary.
- **Age:** you are under 21, or there is no term of at least 6 months that would end while you are no older than 65.
- **Credit record:** your credit report shows a loan in default or serious arrears that has not been resolved.
- **Documents:** a document is missing, out of date, incomplete or could not be verified, or names do not match and we could not resolve why.
- **Suspected fraud:** a document appears to have been altered, or information is false. In these cases we may not be able to give you details.

If we decline, we tell you the main reason, unless the law prevents us or telling you would help someone commit fraud. Being declined by us is not reported as a missed payment and does not mean you owe anything.

## A person makes every loan decision

Every Kaskazi Personal Loan and Kaskazi Salary Advance decision is made by a person in our lending team, never by the banking assistant or by a computer alone.

The system and the assistant do some of the work: they read figures from your documents, run the affordability calculation and flag anything that needs a closer look. But a person reviews the application, checks anything flagged, and decides. If the assistant tells you a loan is likely to fit, that is a guide, not an approval. If you are ever told that the assistant has approved or declined your loan, that is a mistake, and please let us know.

## Asking for a loan decision to be reviewed

If you think a decision on your Kaskazi Personal Loan application is wrong, you can ask for it to be reviewed. You might think we misread a figure, missed an income, counted a loan you have already repaid, or relied on wrong information in your credit report.

1. Ask for a review in the app, at a branch or through the assistant. Say why you think the decision is wrong, and include any new document that supports it, such as proof that a loan has been closed.
2. A more senior member of the lending team who was not involved in the first decision looks at the application again, with the new information.
3. We handle review requests through our complaints process. You get a reference starting KZC- followed by 6 digits. We acknowledge your request within 24 hours and aim to give you an answer within 7 working days. A complex case can take up to 30 days, and we keep you updated.
4. If you are still unhappy, you can ask for the matter to go to the Customer Experience team, and then to the Head of Customer Experience.

If the problem is wrong information in your credit report, we can tell you which bureau the information came from. If the information came from us and is wrong, we correct it.

In a real bank, details of an outside complaints service would appear here. Kaskazi Bank is invented and is not regulated by anyone.

## What to do if your loan application is declined

If your Kaskazi Personal Loan application is declined, you can:

- ask us to explain the reason in more detail, and ask for a review if you disagree
- apply for a smaller amount or a longer term, if affordability was the reason
- apply again once your circumstances change, for example when an existing loan is repaid, your pay rises or you have 3 months of salary history
- fix a document problem, such as an expired ID, and reapply

Making many loan applications in a short time, with us or with other lenders, can count against you, so it is worth understanding why you were declined before you apply again. A member of our team is always happy to talk it through.
