# Staying safe: fraud and scams

Scammers don't usually break into bank systems. They trick people into handing over their PIN, a one-time code or their money. This guide explains the scams Kaskazi Bank customers are most likely to meet, how to recognise them, and exactly what to do if you think you have been caught.

## How scams usually work

Most scams follow the same pattern, whatever story the scammer tells.

- **Urgency.** You are told to act now: your account will be closed, a prize will expire, a relative is in trouble, a payment must be reversed today.
- **Authority or trust.** The scammer pretends to be someone you would normally believe: the bank, your mobile network, the police, an employer, a family member.
- **A request for something secret.** Sooner or later you are asked for a PIN, a one-time code, a password, or to send money.
- **Secrecy.** You are told not to tell anyone, not to hang up, or not to go to a branch.

If you notice any two of these together, stop. A real bank will never be upset if you end a call and contact it yourself through the Kaskazi app, the assistant or a branch.

## What Kaskazi Bank will never ask you for

Kaskazi Bank will never ask you, by phone call, text message, email, social media, chat or in person, for:

- your card PIN
- your full card number, or the security code on the back of your card
- a one-time code sent to your phone
- your password for the Kaskazi app or online banking

We will also never:

- ask you to move money to a "safe account" or a "holding account" to protect it
- ask you to send money to stop your account from being closed
- send someone to your home or office to collect your card, PIN or documents
- ask you to install an app that lets someone else see or control your phone or computer
- ask you to pay a fee upfront before we give you a loan

The Kaskazi assistant will never ask for these either. If anyone asks you for any of them, it is a scam, however convincing they sound, even if they know your name, your ID number, your account number or details of your recent payments. Scammers often have some of your details already.

## Fake calls and messages from "the bank"

A scammer calls, texts or messages you pretending to be from Kaskazi Bank. The caller ID or sender name may even look like ours, because these can be faked. Typical stories:

- "We've noticed suspicious activity on your account. To stop it, read me the code we've just sent you."
- "Your card has been blocked. Confirm your PIN so we can unblock it."
- "Your account is being upgraded. Click this link and log in to keep it open."
- "Your money is at risk. Move it to this safe account while we investigate."

The code they ask for is often a real one-time code, triggered by the scammer at that moment, to approve a payment or log in as you. Reading it out gives them access.

### What to do with a suspicious call or message

- End the call. Don't press any buttons or follow instructions.
- Don't click links in the message.
- Contact us yourself, through the Kaskazi app, the assistant or a branch. Don't use any number or link the caller gave you.
- If you've already shared anything, follow the steps in the section on what to do if you've been scammed.

### Fake customer care accounts on social media

Scammers set up social media pages and chat accounts that copy a bank's name and logo, and reply to people who post complaints. They then ask for your details in private messages. Kaskazi Bank will never ask for your PIN, codes or password in a social media message. Use the Kaskazi app or the assistant to contact us.

## SIM swap fraud

In a SIM swap, a fraudster persuades or bribes someone to move your phone number to a new SIM card that they control. From then on, your calls, messages and one-time codes go to them, not you. They can then try to reset your app password and move your money.

### Warning signs of a SIM swap

- Your phone suddenly shows no network, "SIM not registered" or emergency calls only, in a place where it normally works.
- You get a message that your SIM has been changed or replaced, and you didn't ask for it.
- You stop receiving calls and messages, while friends say your phone is ringing.
- Someone calls asking you to switch your phone off or restart it "for an upgrade". This keeps you from noticing the swap.

### What to do if you suspect a SIM swap

1. Contact your mobile network straight away from another phone and ask them to stop the swap and block the new SIM.
2. Block your Kaskazi cards in the app or through the assistant, from another device if needed.
3. Tell us, through the assistant, the contact centre (number not available in this demonstration) or a branch, so we can check your account and secure your app.
4. Once your number is back in your hands, change your Kaskazi app password.

Your one-time codes, including the code to unblock a card, only go to your registered phone number. If your phone number changes, update it at a branch with your national ID card or passport.

## Fake prizes, refunds and "money sent by mistake"

These scams promise you money, or claim that someone sent you money by mistake.

### Fake prizes and promotions

You're told you've won a prize, a phone or a cash promotion from a bank, a mobile network or a radio station. To claim it you must pay a "processing fee", share a code, or give your card details. Real promotions don't ask you to pay to receive a prize, and Kaskazi Bank never asks for a code or PIN to give you anything.

### Fake refunds

A caller says you are owed a refund, for example from overcharged fees, a cancelled order or tax, and asks for your card number and a one-time code "to send the refund". The code actually approves a payment out of your account. Real refunds from Kaskazi Bank go straight into your account. We never need a code from you to pay you.

### "I sent you money by mistake"

You get a message that looks like a payment notification saying money has arrived in your account or mobile money wallet. Then someone calls, apologises, and asks you to send it back.

Often, no money arrived. The message was fake. Sometimes the money did arrive, but it was stolen from someone else, and it will be reversed later, leaving you out of pocket.

- Check your balance yourself in the Kaskazi app, not from a message.
- Don't send money back to anyone who asks. If money really was sent to you by mistake, the sender's bank or wallet provider will handle the reversal through proper channels.

## Impostor agents

Some scammers pretend to be bank agents, or work alongside a real agent shop, to get your PIN or cash.

- A real agent never needs your PIN. You enter it yourself, privately.
- A real agent never keeps your card or ID out of your sight.
- Deposits at a Kaskazi agent are free. If someone asks for a fee to deposit money, that isn't right.
- Agent withdrawals have published fees, set out below. If an agent asks for more, don't go ahead, and tell us.

| Agent withdrawal amount | Kaskazi fee |
|---|---|
| Up to KES 2,500 | KES 30 |
| Up to KES 5,000 | KES 50 |
| Up to KES 10,000 | KES 80 |
| Up to KES 20,000 | KES 120 |
| Up to KES 50,000 | KES 200 |

A balance enquiry at an agent costs KES 10 and a mini statement costs KES 15. You can take out up to KES 50,000 in one agent withdrawal, and up to KES 100,000 a day at agents.

Be wary of anyone near an agent or ATM who offers to "help" you. Never hand your card, phone or PIN to a stranger.

## Staying safe at ATMs

Fraudsters at ATMs try to see your PIN and then get hold of your card, or fit devices to the machine that copy your card's details.

- **Look at the machine before you use it.** If the card slot looks loose, bulky or different from usual, or there is something stuck over the keypad, don't use it. Use another ATM and tell the bank that owns it.
- **Cover the keypad** with your other hand every time you type your PIN, even if nobody seems to be near.
- **Don't accept help.** A stranger who says the machine is "tricky" and offers to help is a common trick. Cancel, take your card and leave.
- **If the ATM keeps your card,** don't walk away to find help while a stranger waits by the machine. Block the card as lost or stolen in the Kaskazi app or through the assistant straight away, while you are still standing there.
- **If cash doesn't come out** but the money leaves your account, dispute the withdrawal. Our document on disputing a card payment explains how.

Using a Kaskazi ATM is free, and you can check your balance free in the Kaskazi app instead of at an ATM.

## Phishing links and fake websites

Phishing is a message, email or advert with a link to a fake page that looks like a bank's login page. When you type in your details, the scammer gets them.

- Don't log in to Kaskazi banking through a link in a message, email or advert. Open the Kaskazi app yourself.
- Our official web address is not available in this demonstration.
- Look out for small misspellings, odd endings to web addresses, and pages that ask for your PIN or a one-time code to "verify" your account. A real login never asks for your card PIN.
- Only install the Kaskazi app from your phone's official app store. Never install an app from a link someone sends you.

### Fake loan offers

A message or advert offers a quick loan, "approved" without checks, if you first pay a processing or insurance fee. Once you pay, the lender disappears. Kaskazi Bank never asks for a fee before paying out a loan. For a Kaskazi Personal Loan, for example, the arrangement fee is deducted from the loan before it is paid to you, so you never pay anything upfront.

### Fake payments to sellers

If you sell something online, a "buyer" may send a fake payment message, or claim to have overpaid and ask you to refund the difference. Before you hand over goods, check in the Kaskazi app that the money has actually arrived in your account.

## The new-recipient limit: KES 50,000 in the first 24 hours

When you add a new recipient, someone you haven't paid before, you can send them up to KES 50,000 in total in the first 24 hours. After 24 hours, your normal transfer limits apply.

This limit exists because scammers almost always push people to send large amounts quickly to an account they've never used. The 24 hours give you time to think, check, and talk to someone you trust. If you realise you've been tricked, you can tell us before more money goes.

### Example: paying a new landlord

You move house and need to pay KES 120,000 to your new landlord, whom you haven't paid before.

- Today you can send up to KES 50,000.
- Once 24 hours have passed since you added the landlord, you can send the remaining KES 70,000.

If you know you'll need to pay a new recipient a large amount, add them a day ahead. The new-recipient limit sits within your overall digital daily limit of KES 500,000.

If someone insists that you must pay a large amount to a new account today and can't wait, treat that as a warning sign.

## Protecting your Kaskazi app and one-time codes

A few built-in protections help keep your Kaskazi app safe:

- **One-time codes** work for 5 minutes only. Each code is for one action, and the message says what it is for. Read the message before you use the code.
- **Login lock:** after 5 wrong login attempts, the app is locked, to stop someone guessing your password.
- **Session timeout:** if you leave the app open without using it for 5 minutes, you are logged out.

You can help too:

- Use a password that you don't use anywhere else.
- Lock your phone with a PIN, pattern or fingerprint, and don't share it.
- Don't let anyone else use your phone to "help" you with your banking.
- If you get a one-time code you didn't ask for, someone may be trying to log in or pay as you. Don't share it. Tell us.

## If you think you've been scammed: what to do now

Act straight away. Minutes can matter.

### Step 1: block your card

Block your Kaskazi Debit Card or Kaskazi Credit Card in the Kaskazi app or by asking the assistant. You can do this at any time of day or night, and card blocks never wait for staff, because you do them yourself.

- If you're not sure what the scammer has, use a temporary block. You can unblock it later with a one-time code.
- If you gave away your card number, security code, PIN or a one-time code, block the card as lost or stolen. It is cancelled for good, and you can order a replacement for KES 500.

### Step 2: report it to us

Tell us what happened as soon as you can:

- through the assistant, which can hand you over to a person; during staffed hours (7:00 am to 10:00 pm, every day) we aim to do this within 5 minutes
- through our contact centre, open 24 hours a day, every day (number not available in this demonstration)
- at any branch

Outside staffed hours, your conversation with the assistant waits in the queue and a person replies when the team is next in. That's why Step 1 comes first: you don't need anyone's help to block a card.

Tell us what you shared, what payments you made or saw, and when. If you sent money to a scammer, give us the recipient's details and the time.

### Step 3: secure your app and phone

- Change your Kaskazi app password.
- If you installed an app at the scammer's request, uninstall it, and don't use the phone for banking until it has been checked.
- If you think your SIM has been swapped, contact your mobile network straight away.

### Step 4: keep the evidence

Keep call logs, messages, screenshots, links and the numbers or accounts the scammer used. Don't delete the conversation. You may also want to report the scam to the police.

## Getting money back after a scam

What happens next depends on how the money left your account.

### Card payments you didn't authorise

If a scammer used your Kaskazi card or card details:

- before you reported it, the most you lose is KES 5,000, unless you were careless with your PIN or a one-time code
- after you reported it, you lose nothing (KES 0)

Report the payments as a card dispute within 60 days of the transaction date. Our document on disputing a card payment explains the process and what counts as carelessness.

Sharing a one-time code or your PIN with a scammer counts as carelessness, even when the scammer was very convincing. That is why the most important rule in this guide is never to share them.

### Money you sent yourself

If you were tricked into sending money yourself, by transfer or to a mobile money wallet, it is harder to get back, because you approved the payment. Tell us straight away. We will contact the bank or wallet provider that received it and ask it to hold the money, but we can't promise to recover it. The sooner you tell us, the better the chance.

## Helping family members stay safe

Older relatives, and people new to mobile banking, are often targeted. You can help by talking through this guide with them, especially these points:

- Kaskazi Bank never asks for a PIN, full card number, one-time code or password.
- It's always fine to end a call and contact the bank yourself.
- Never move money to a "safe account".
- Pause before paying a new recipient. The KES 50,000 limit in the first 24 hours is there to help.

If a family member has been scammed, help them block their card in the app or through the assistant first, then report it.
