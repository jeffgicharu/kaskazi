# Complaint handling procedure (staff)

## Purpose and scope of the complaint handling procedure

This procedure tells Kaskazi Bank staff how to handle a customer complaint from the moment it is raised to the moment it is closed. It applies to everyone who deals with customers: branch staff, the contact centre, the team that takes over conversations from the Kaskazi assistant, agency banking staff and the Customer Experience team.

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction from a customer, or someone acting for them, about our products, service, fees, decisions, staff, agents or the assistant, where they expect a response. The customer does not need to use the word "complaint". If in doubt, treat it as one: logging a complaint that turns out to be a simple query costs little; missing a real complaint costs the customer's trust.

The customer-facing version of this process is "How to make a complaint". What we tell customers there is what we must deliver here.

## Complaint timelines at a glance

Every complaint is held to these timelines. They start when the complaint is first received, through any channel, not when it reaches the right team.

| Stage | Timeline |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge | Within 24 hours |
| Resolve a standard complaint | Within 7 working days |
| Resolve a complex complaint | Within 30 days |
| Card dispute: acknowledge | Within 24 hours |
| Card dispute: provisional credit for amounts up to KES 20,000 | Within 10 working days |
| Card dispute: investigation | Up to 45 days |
| Request for a copy of personal data | Respond within 7 days |

A working day is Monday to Friday, except public holidays.

## Step 1: Logging a complaint

Log every complaint in the complaints system at the first contact, before you start trying to fix it, even if you expect to resolve it on the spot.

**What to record:**

- the customer's name and the account or card concerned (masked as the system shows it),
- the channel: assistant handover, app form, branch, contact centre or in writing,
- the date and time the complaint was received,
- what the customer says went wrong, in their words as far as possible,
- what the customer wants us to do, and
- any transaction references, dates and amounts.

**The reference.** The system issues a reference in the form "KZC-" followed by 6 digits. Give it to the customer straight away and tell them to quote it in every contact.

**Complaints from the assistant.** When the assistant logs a complaint, the reference and the conversation summary are already in the system. Check the summary against the transcript and correct it if needed.

**Never ask for a PIN, password, one-time code or full card number** to log or investigate a complaint. Our systems never need them.

## Step 2: Categorising a complaint

Choose one main category and any flags that apply. Categories drive routing and the root-cause reports, so choose the one that best describes the customer's experience, not the team you think is at fault.

**Main categories:** accounts; savings and deposits; cards; card disputes; loans and credit; payments and transfers; digital banking (app, online banking, USSD); the Kaskazi assistant; agents; fees and charges; service and staff conduct; data and privacy.

**Flags:**

- **Fraud or scam.** The customer may have lost money to fraud. Route at once to the fraud team and protect the account first.
- **Vulnerable customer.** Bereavement, financial hardship, illness, disability or anything that makes the customer less able to protect their interests. Handle with extra care and flexibility.
- **Data.** The complaint involves the customer's personal information.
- **Repeat.** The customer has complained about the same issue before. Link the earlier reference.
- **Complex.** The complaint cannot reasonably be resolved within 7 working days, for example because we need information from another bank, a mobile money provider or an agent, or because many transactions are involved.

A complaint can be flagged complex when it is logged or later, but you must tell the customer why on the day you flag it.

## Step 3: Acknowledging a complaint within 24 hours

Every complaint must be acknowledged within 24 hours of being received. If you resolve the complaint fully within 24 hours, your resolution message also serves as the acknowledgement.

**The acknowledgement must include:**

- the KZC- reference,
- a short summary of the complaint as we understand it,
- who is handling it (team, not personal phone numbers),
- when the customer can expect our answer: within 7 working days, or within 30 days if it is complex, with the reason, and
- how to correct our understanding if we have got it wrong.

**Tone.** Write plainly and personally. Thank the customer for telling us. Do not admit or deny fault at this stage; say we are looking into it.

## Step 4: Investigating a complaint

Investigate fairly, using the evidence, and keep notes in the complaint record as you go so that anyone picking it up can see what has been done.

**Gather the evidence:**

- transaction records, fee lines and statements,
- the relevant document: the tariff guide, product terms or procedure in force on the date of the event,
- assistant transcripts, the sources it cited and the actions it took,
- agent device logs and confirmation messages for agent complaints,
- notes from the branch or contact centre, and
- information from other banks or providers where the payment left Kaskazi.

**Check against our own standards.** Was the fee the one in the tariff? Was a waiver missed, for example the Everyday Account monthly fee of KES 250 when salary was paid in or the average balance was KES 50,000 or more? Did we meet our service standards, such as the branch queue target of 15 minutes, the contact centre answer target of 60 seconds, or the assistant handover target of 5 minutes during staffed hours?

**Talk to the customer** if anything is unclear. Contact them through the channel they used, or the one they asked for.

**Keep the customer informed.** If the investigation is taking time, update the customer before they need to chase us, and always before a promised date passes.

## Step 5: Resolving a complaint within the timelines

Resolve standard complaints within 7 working days and complex complaints within 30 days of receipt.

**Resolve on the spot where you can.** If a fee was charged in error and the evidence is clear, refund it during the conversation, within your authority, and record it. The customer still gets a reference and a written outcome.

**Decide the outcome.** Decide whether the complaint is upheld (we got it wrong), partly upheld, or not upheld, and why.

**Put things right.** Where we were at fault, aim to put the customer back in the position they would have been in. This can include refunding fees, correcting interest, reversing a charge, fixing a record, and an apology. Redress beyond your authority must be approved by your team leader before you promise it.

**Write the final response.** It must say what we found, our decision and why, what we are doing to put it right, and how the customer can escalate if they are not happy. Use plain English and the customer's preferred language, English or Swahili.

**If you will miss a deadline,** escalate to your team leader before it passes, tell the customer why, and give a new date.

## Step 6: Escalating a complaint

There are three escalation levels. A customer can ask to move to the next level at any time, without giving a reason, and you must not discourage them.

1. **The agent or branch who handled it.** The first person or branch to receive the complaint owns it and tries to resolve it.
2. **The Customer Experience team.** Escalate when the customer asks, when the complaint is outside your authority, when you cannot resolve it within the timeline, or when it involves staff conduct. The team reviews the complaint afresh.
3. **The Head of Customer Experience.** Escalate when the customer is not satisfied with the Customer Experience team's answer. The Head of Customer Experience gives the bank's final answer.

**When escalating,** update the record with a summary of what has been done, the evidence and why you are escalating, so the next level does not have to ask the customer to repeat it. The timelines keep running from the date the complaint was first received.

## Step 7: Closing a complaint with the customer's agreement

Close a complaint only when the customer agrees it has been resolved. Sending a final response is not enough.

- **Ask the customer** whether they are satisfied with the outcome and whether there is anything else.
- **If they agree,** record their agreement, the outcome and any redress paid, and close the complaint.
- **If they do not agree,** keep the complaint open and offer the next escalation level.
- **If the customer does not reply** to a final response, follow up once through their preferred channel and record the attempt before closing it as resolved without agreement.

**Final answers from the Head of Customer Experience** must confirm that the bank's own process has ended and include this note: "In a real bank, details of the regulator's complaints service would be here. Kaskazi Bank is invented and is not regulated by anyone."

## Step 8: Recording the root cause

Every closed complaint must have a root cause recorded. Root causes are how we stop the same thing happening again.

**Root-cause categories:**

- **Process:** a procedure was unclear, missing or not followed.
- **System:** an error in the app, core banking or another system.
- **People:** a staff or agent error, or conduct.
- **Product or pricing:** the product or fee works as designed, but the design causes harm or confusion.
- **Communication:** the customer was not told something, or was told it unclearly.
- **Documentation:** a bank document was wrong, unclear or missing. If the assistant gave a wrong answer from a document, record the document and section and notify the document's owner team.
- **Third party:** another bank, provider or agent caused the problem.
- **No fault:** the bank acted correctly; the complaint was not upheld.

Add a short note on what should change. The Customer Experience team reviews root causes regularly and reports recurring themes to the teams that own them.

## Special cases in complaint handling

**Card disputes** follow the dispute timelines: customers must report within 60 days of the transaction date; acknowledge within 24 hours; give provisional credit within 10 working days for disputes up to KES 20,000; complete the investigation within 45 days. A complaint about how a dispute was handled follows this procedure.

**Fraud and scams.** Protect the account first: help the customer block their card and secure their app. Customer liability for card fraud before reporting is at most KES 5,000, unless they were careless with their PIN or code, and KES 0 after reporting.

**Money sent to the wrong person.** Start the recovery request straight away; the sooner, the better the chance. Never promise the money will be returned: we cannot take money from another account without the holder's agreement or a lawful order.

**Data requests and privacy.** Requests for a copy of personal data must be answered within 7 days. Route privacy complaints to the data protection team on the day they are received.

**Complaints about a colleague.** Never investigate a complaint about yourself or a colleague you manage. Escalate it to the Customer Experience team.
