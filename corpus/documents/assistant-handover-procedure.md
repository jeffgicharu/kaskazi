# Taking over a conversation from the assistant (staff)

## Purpose of the assistant handover procedure

This procedure tells Kaskazi Bank staff how to take over a customer conversation from the Kaskazi assistant, the AI chat assistant in the app and online banking. It covers why conversations are handed over, what you see when one arrives, how to verify the customer, how to reply, how to hand back or close, and how to record the outcome.

The assistant answers questions from the bank's documents, cites them, and can carry out some tasks, always after the customer confirms. It is designed to hand over to a person whenever a person would serve the customer better. When it does, the customer should feel no gap: you pick up where it left off, without asking them to repeat themselves.

## Why the assistant hands a conversation over

The assistant hands a conversation to the handover queue for one of these reasons, which is shown on the handover:

- **The customer asked for a person.** Customers can ask at any time, for any reason. The assistant never argues or tries to keep them.
- **Low confidence.** The assistant could not find a clear answer in the bank's documents, or the documents it found did not agree, and it would rather pass the question on than guess.
- **Possible fraud or a scam.** The customer reports a payment they did not make, says they were tricked into paying, has shared a PIN or code, or describes a caller asking them to move money.
- **Bereavement.** The customer says an account holder has died, or is dealing with a loss.
- **Financial hardship.** The customer says they cannot make a loan or credit card payment, has lost their income, or is in money difficulty.
- **Repeated failed checks.** The customer has failed identity checks more than once, for example one-time codes that did not match.
- **A complaint that needs a person,** for example one the customer wants to discuss, or one about the assistant itself.

Sensitive reasons (fraud, bereavement, hardship and failed checks) are marked so you can see them before you open the conversation.

## The handover queue and the 5-minute target

Our target is to reply to every handover within 5 minutes during staffed hours, which are 7:00 am to 10:00 pm, every day.

- **Order of the queue.** Fraud and scam handovers go to the top of the queue, because minutes matter when money is moving. Other sensitive handovers come next; then all others, oldest first.
- **Outside staffed hours,** the conversation waits in the queue and a person replies when the team is next in. The assistant tells the customer this. Customers can still block their own card at any time in the app or through the assistant; card blocks never wait for a person.
- **At the start of a shift,** work through conversations waiting from overnight in queue order. Open with an apology for the wait.
- **If you cannot reply within the target,** for example because the queue is long, tell your team leader so more people can join.

## What you see when you take over

When you accept a handover, the handover panel shows you everything the assistant knew and did, so you do not need to ask the customer to start again.

- **The reason for the handover**, from the list above.
- **A summary**, written by the assistant: who the customer is, what they asked, what it answered, and what is still open.
- **The masked transcript** of the whole conversation. Account numbers, card numbers, phone numbers and any codes the customer typed are masked, showing only the last few digits or nothing at all.
- **The sources used**: each bank document and section the assistant cited in its answers, so you can check what it told the customer.
- **The tool calls**: every action the assistant took or looked up, such as a balance check, a card block or a payment it prepared, with the result and whether the customer confirmed it.
- **The customer's sign-in status**: whether they are signed in to the app or online banking, and whether they have passed a one-time code check in this conversation.

**Read before you reply.** Read the summary, skim the transcript and check the sources and tool calls. If the assistant gave an answer that was wrong or incomplete, you will correct it in your reply.

## Verifying the customer in a handover

You must be sure who you are talking to before you discuss or change anything on an account.

**Signed-in customers.** A customer chatting in the app or online banking has already signed in, so they have been identified by their sign-in. You can discuss their accounts and help with routine tasks.

**Extra check for sensitive actions.** Before you make a sensitive change, such as changing contact details, or when the conversation suggests someone else may be using the phone, send a one-time code from the handover panel. It goes to the customer's registered phone number and is valid for 5 minutes. The customer enters it on the app's own verification screen, and the panel shows you whether it passed. You never see the code, and you must never ask the customer to type it into the chat.

**Repeated failed checks.** If the customer fails the check more than once, stop account-specific help. Do not explain which detail was wrong. Tell the customer, kindly, that for their security we need to see them at a branch with their national ID card or passport, and record the outcome. If you suspect fraud, flag the conversation to the fraud team.

**Customers who are not signed in** can get general information only. For anything about their accounts, ask them to sign in to the app or online banking, or visit a branch.

## Never ask for a PIN or a full card number

Never ask a customer, in any words, for their card PIN, app PIN, password, one-time code, full card number or the security code on the back of their card. We never need them, and customers are told that anyone who asks is not genuine. If you ask, you undo that protection.

**If the customer offers it anyway,** stop them before they send it. If they have already typed it, the transcript masks it, but treat it as exposed:

- for a PIN, help them block the card and advise them to change the PIN,
- for a password or app PIN, advise them to change it straight away,
- for a one-time code, tell them never to share codes and check whether anything was changed, and
- for a full card number, check for unrecognised payments and offer to block the card.

Record what happened in the outcome notes.

## Replying to the customer

Your first message should show the customer that a person has taken over and that you know what they need.

- **Introduce yourself** by first name and say you are from Kaskazi Bank.
- **Show you have read the conversation**, for example: "I can see your instant transfer of KES 45,000 this morning has not arrived. Let me look into it."
- **Use plain words**, in English or Swahili, matching the customer.
- **Quote the bank's documents** for figures, as the assistant does, and give the document's name if the customer may want to check it. Never quote a figure from memory.
- **Correct the assistant openly** if it was wrong: "The assistant's earlier answer wasn't right. The correct fee is…"
- **Confirm before you act.** Like the assistant, state exactly what you are about to do and get the customer's clear agreement before you change anything.
- **Promise only what you can deliver.** If you cannot fix it in the chat, say what will happen next and when, using our published timelines.

## Handling sensitive situations in a handover

**Possible fraud or a scam.** Act first, then investigate. Help the customer block their card (they can also do it themselves), secure their app, and stop any payment we still can. Log the case with a KZC- reference and refer it to the fraud team. Reassure them: the most a customer loses for card fraud before reporting is KES 5,000, unless they were careless with their PIN or code, and KES 0 after reporting.

**Bereavement.** Offer condolences and do not rush. Explain, in general terms, what the bank will need and that it can be dealt with at a branch. Do not discuss the deceased person's accounts in detail in the chat.

**Financial hardship.** Listen and take it seriously. Do not offer new credit as a solution. Explain what happens if a payment is missed, for example the personal loan late payment fee of KES 500 for each missed instalment or the credit card late payment fee of KES 1,000, and refer the customer to the lending team to discuss their options. Log it so the lending team can contact them.

**Distress or vulnerability.** If the customer seems confused, distressed or at risk, slow down, keep messages short, and involve your team leader.

## Handing back to the assistant or closing the conversation

When the reason for the handover has been dealt with, either hand the conversation back to the assistant or close it.

**Handing back.** Hand back when the customer has more routine questions that the assistant can answer, and the customer is happy to continue with it. Tell them, for example: "I'll pass you back to the assistant for that. You can ask for a person again at any time." Add a short note, which the assistant reads so it continues with the right context. Never hand back a fraud, bereavement, hardship or failed-check conversation.

**Closing.** Close the conversation when the customer has nothing else. Before you close:

- summarise what you did and anything still to happen, with dates,
- give any reference, such as a KZC- complaint reference, and
- ask whether there is anything else.

If the customer stops replying, send one message saying how to continue (they can reply in the same chat) and close the conversation.

## Recording the outcome of a handover

Every handover must have its outcome recorded before you take the next one.

**Choose an outcome:**

- resolved in the chat,
- handed back to the assistant,
- complaint logged (add the KZC- reference),
- referred to a branch,
- referred to another team (fraud, lending, cards or data protection), or
- closed without reply from the customer.

**Add notes** in plain words: what the customer needed, what you did, and anything still open.

**Flag the assistant's answer** if it was wrong, incomplete or missed a better source. Choose the document and section concerned so the owner team can fix it. Also flag cases where it handed over for low confidence but the answer was clearly in the documents, so the retrieval can be improved.

**If the customer was unhappy** with the service, log a complaint under the complaint handling procedure, even if you resolved the issue.
