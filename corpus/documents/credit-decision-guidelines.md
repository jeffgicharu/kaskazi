# Credit decision guidelines (staff)

These guidelines are for Kaskazi Bank staff who review, approve or decline Kaskazi Personal Loan applications and confirm Kaskazi Salary Advances. They set out who may decide what, the checks a credit reviewer must complete, when to decline, how to record reasons, and the standards of fairness and integrity that apply to every decision. They sit alongside the Document verification procedure (staff), which covers how flagged documents are checked before a credit decision.

Kaskazi Bank is invented. This procedure was written for a demonstration of a banking assistant and governs no real lending.

## Principles behind every credit decision

Every Kaskazi credit decision rests on four principles:

1. **A person decides.** Every approval, decline and counter-offer is made by a named member of staff who holds the authority for it. No decision is made by the banking assistant or by the system alone.
2. **Affordable lending only.** We lend only what the customer can repay without hardship. The rules in these guidelines exist to protect customers as much as the bank.
3. **Consistency.** Customers in the same position get the same answer. Judgement is used where the rules allow it, and the reason is always written down.
4. **Fairness and integrity.** Decisions are explained honestly, made without pressure or favour, and free of conflicts of interest.

## Approval authority: who may approve what

Credit approval authority is personal. It is delegated in writing to a named individual by the Head of Credit, recorded in the lending system, and can be reduced or withdrawn at any time. Authority belongs to the person, not to their job title or branch, and it cannot be passed to anyone else, even temporarily.

The authority structure for the Kaskazi Personal Loan is:

- **Credit reviewers** may approve loans up to the individual limit in their delegation letter.
- **Senior credit reviewers** may approve loans up to their own, higher, individual limit.
- **The Head of Credit** may approve loans up to the Head of Credit's limit.
- **The Credit Committee** decides any loan above the highest individual limit, and any case referred to it.

The amounts for each limit are set in each holder's delegation letter and in the lending system. They are not published in this document. Whatever the limits, no one can approve more than the product maximum of KES 3,000,000.

Rules that apply at every level:

- The lending system blocks an approval above your limit. If an application is above your limit, complete your checks, record your recommendation and refer it to someone with enough authority. Never split one customer's borrowing into smaller loans to bring it within your limit.
- A counter-offer for a smaller amount needs authority for the amount offered.
- You may decline any application that fails a hard rule in the next section, whatever the amount. A decline made on judgement, for example because of the customer's credit record, needs authority for the amount applied for.
- The member of staff who took the application with the customer, at a branch or in a conversation handed over from the assistant, may not also approve it.
- **Kaskazi Salary Advance** confirmations are made by staff who hold salary advance authority. The product rules, not a separate amount limit, set what can be confirmed.

### The banking assistant has no authority

The banking assistant has no credit authority of any kind and can never be given any. It may explain products, quote figures, prepare an application, summarise documents and run the affordability calculation, but its output is working material, not a decision or evidence. Never record "approved by the assistant" or rely on the assistant's summary instead of checking the source documents yourself.

If a customer believes the assistant approved or declined their loan, correct this politely: explain that a person in the lending team makes every decision, and tell them where their application stands.

## Hard rules that cannot be overridden

These rules apply to every Kaskazi Personal Loan. No one at any level of authority may approve an application that breaks one, and there is no exceptions process for them.

| Rule | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Loan amount | KES 50,000 to KES 3,000,000 |
| Term | 6 months to 60 months |
| Age | At least 21 at application; no older than 65 when the loan ends |
| Net monthly pay | At least KES 25,000 |
| Salary history | At least 3 months; 6 months of statements if salary is paid at another bank |
| Affordability | All monthly loan repayments, including the new one, no more than 40% of net monthly pay |
| Documents | All required documents received and verified, with no open flags |
| Account | A Kaskazi Everyday Account in the customer's name to receive the loan |

The pricing is also fixed: 15.5% a year (the Kaskazi Base Rate of 11.5% plus a margin of 4.0 percentage points), an arrangement fee of 2.5% with a minimum of KES 2,000, and credit life insurance of 0.5% of the loan for each year of the term, part years counted as full years. Reviewers may not change the rate, waive or discount the arrangement fee, or remove the insurance.

## The checks a credit reviewer completes

Complete every check below for every Kaskazi Personal Loan application, and record the result of each in the lending system. A check that does not apply, such as statements for a customer paid into Kaskazi, is recorded as not applicable, not left blank.

1. **Identity.** The customer's identity checks are complete, the ID or passport is valid and not expired, and the name matches across the application, the documents and the account.
2. **Age and term.** The customer is at least 21, and the last instalment falls before their 66th birthday. If the requested term runs past that, the longest allowed term is what can be offered.
3. **Amount and term.** Within KES 50,000 to KES 3,000,000 and 6 months to 60 months.
4. **Net monthly pay.** Work it out from the latest 3 payslips. Use regular pay only; leave out one-off bonuses and arrears. Where pay varies, use the amount shown regularly, not the highest month. Add back any loan repayment the employer deducts, then count it as a repayment. Net monthly pay must be at least KES 25,000.
5. **Salary history.** At least 3 months of salary, either credited to a Kaskazi account or shown on 6 months of statements from the other bank. Confirm the net pay on each payslip arrives in the account.
6. **Documents.** Every document has the status verified under the Document verification procedure (staff), and no flag is open. Do not decide while any document is still under review.
7. **Existing commitments.** Build the full list of monthly loan repayments from three sources: the application, the payslips and the credit reference bureau report. Include loans from banks, savings and credit cooperatives, employers and mobile or digital lenders. For a credit card, count the minimum monthly payment: 5.0% of the balance, with a floor of KES 1,000, or the full balance if lower. Where the customer is clearing a loan with the new money, it may be left out only if the payoff is made from the disbursement and proof of closure is obtained.
8. **Affordability.** Recalculate it yourself; do not rely only on the system's or the assistant's figure. Total monthly repayments, including the new instalment, must be no more than 40% of net monthly pay.
9. **Credit reference bureau report.** Review the report for loans in default, serious or recent arrears, written-off debts, and loans the customer did not declare. In this demonstration the bureau check is simulated.
10. **Conduct with Kaskazi.** Review the customer's accounts and any loans or advances with us, including any Kaskazi Salary Advance collected late.
11. **Consistency.** The purpose, amount, employer, pay and account activity tell a consistent story. Investigate anything that does not fit.
12. **Fraud and tampering indicators.** Any flag raised during document verification has been resolved. Unresolved suspicion of tampering means no approval.
13. **Vulnerability.** Note any sign that the customer may be vulnerable, such as recent bereavement, serious illness, or signs of pressure from another person, and take it into account as described below.
14. **The offer.** Before approving, confirm the offer figures in the system: the amount, term, rate, arrangement fee, insurance, amount received, instalment, instalment date, total repaid and total cost of credit.

## When to decline a loan application

Decline a Kaskazi Personal Loan application when:

- it fails any hard rule, and no counter-offer within the rules would work or the customer does not want one
- the credit reference bureau report shows a loan in default or serious arrears that has not been resolved, or a pattern of recent missed payments
- a document cannot be verified, or the customer cannot provide an acceptable replacement
- there is evidence that a document has been altered, or that information given is false
- the application does not hold together, for example the account activity does not support the stated employment, and the customer cannot explain it
- you judge that the loan would cause the customer hardship even though it falls within 40%, for example because of a known change in their circumstances

Do not decline only because of an old, settled problem on the credit report, because a name differs in a way that has been explained, or because the customer is new to Kaskazi.

## When to make a counter-offer instead of declining

If the application fails only on affordability, or the term breaks the age rule, offer what does fit before declining. Work out the largest loan within the 40% limit for the requested term, and also for a longer term, and tell the customer about both. Remind them that a longer term lowers the instalment but costs more interest overall. Never push the customer to take the largest amount that fits, or a longer term than they need.

## Recording the decision and the reasons

Record every decision in the lending system before you tell the customer. The record must include:

- the decision: approved, approved on a counter-offer, or declined
- your name, and the authority under which you decided
- the result of each check in the checklist
- the affordability figures: net monthly pay, each existing repayment, the new instalment and the total as a percentage of net pay
- the main reason code, and a free-text explanation in plain language
- any document field you relied on that was corrected during verification, and who corrected it
- any conflict of interest declared, and who the case was passed to

Write the explanation as if the customer will read it, because they can ask for the information we hold about them, and we respond within 7 days. Be factual, specific and respectful. "Total repayments would be 45% of net pay, above the 40% limit" is a good reason. "Customer seems unreliable" is not.

## Telling the customer about a credit decision

Tell the customer the decision promptly, in the app, and give the main reason in plain language. For a counter-offer, explain what we can offer and why. For a decline, explain how to ask for a review.

Do not give reasons that would help someone commit fraud, and do not disclose a fraud suspicion. In those cases, say that we could not verify the application and are unable to proceed. Never tell a customer an application was declined "by the system" or "by the computer".

The target is a decision within 2 working days of all documents being received and in order. If you cannot meet it, for example because the case needs to go to someone with higher authority, tell the customer and give them an update.

## Treating customers fairly in credit decisions

- **No discrimination.** Do not take into account a customer's tribe, religion, gender, marital status, disability, place of origin or any other personal characteristic that has no bearing on whether they can repay.
- **No pressure.** Never hurry a customer to accept an offer, or encourage them to borrow more than they asked for.
- **Clear costs.** Make sure the customer understands the amount they will receive after the fee and insurance, the instalment and the total they will repay.
- **Vulnerable customers.** Give extra time and explanation. If a customer seems to be under pressure from someone else to borrow, speak to them alone and refer the case to your team leader before deciding.
- **Honest reasons.** Give the real reason for a decision, not a vague or convenient one.

## Conflicts of interest in credit decisions

You must not review, recommend, approve or decline an application, or influence anyone who does, if you have a personal interest in it. That includes applications from:

- yourself
- a member of your family or household
- a close friend, or someone you have a business or financial relationship with
- a colleague who reports to you or to whom you report

If you are given such a case, declare the conflict in the lending system straight away and pass the case to your team leader for reallocation. Applications from Kaskazi staff are decided by the central credit team, never in the applicant's own branch or team.

Never accept a gift, payment or favour connected with a credit decision. If a customer or anyone else offers you one, decline it and report it to your team leader. Any attempt to influence a decision, by anyone, including a colleague, must be reported.

## Reviews of credit decisions

When a customer asks for a decision to be reviewed, the request is handled through the complaints process, with a reference starting KZC- followed by 6 digits. The customer is told we will acknowledge within 24 hours, and we aim to answer within 7 working days, or up to 30 days for a complex case.

The review is carried out by someone more senior than the original decision-maker, who was not involved in the first decision and has authority for the amount. The reviewer looks at the application afresh, including any new information, and records the outcome and the reason in the same way as an original decision. If the original decision was based on wrong information, correct it, including anything we reported to a credit reference bureau.
